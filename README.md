## 1. Project Overview

Artificial Intelligence is increasingly influencing how governments, businesses, and societies operate. However, the benefits of AI depend not only on technological advancement but also on the policies, institutions, safeguards, and inclusive practices that guide its development and use.

This project, *Responsible AI Readiness in Africa*, examines how prepared African countries are to develop, deploy, and govern AI responsibly.

Using data from the Global Index on Responsible AI (GIRAI) 2026, our team analysed 39 African countries to explore differences in responsible AI readiness, identify areas of relative strength and weakness, and highlight governance gaps requiring attention.

We developed an interactive, multi-page Power BI dashboard to transform the index data into accessible insights for decision-makers, researchers, policymakers, and other stakeholders interested in responsible AI in Africa.

The dashboard explores five key dimensions of responsible AI:

1. Inclusion and Diversity
2. Ethics and Sustainability
3. Labour and Skills
4. AI in Public Service
5. Trust and Safety

It also examines governance pillars, regional performance, country rankings, and individual indicators to provide a broader picture of the continent's responsible AI readiness.

This project was completed as part of the DATAIT Bootcamp challenge, where our team of four emerged as winners. I served as the team lead, contributing to the team's analytical direction and project delivery.

### Project Objectives

The main objectives were to:

- Assess responsible AI readiness across 39 African countries.
- Compare countries using their overall GIRAI scores.
- Identify the strongest and weakest dimensions of responsible AI readiness.
- Examine regional differences across Africa.
- Investigate the relationship between enabling conditions and AI policy.
- Evaluate the contribution of civil society engagement to responsible AI governance.
- Identify indicators that may require priority intervention.
- Develop evidence-based recommendations for strengthening responsible AI readiness.
- Communicate complex index data through interactive Power BI visualisations.

## 2. Executive Summary

Our analysis revealed considerable differences in responsible AI readiness across the 39 African countries included in the dashboard.

The average GIRAI score for the African countries in our analysis was **21.79**. Nigeria recorded the highest score at **45.93**, while South Sudan recorded the lowest at **4.04**.

These differences highlight the uneven distribution of responsible AI readiness across the countries examined.

At the dimension level, **Trust and Safety recorded the highest average score at 26.36**, followed by Ethics and Sustainability at 23.38 and Labour and Skills at 23.30. Inclusion and Diversity recorded the lowest average at 16.68, making it the weakest of the five dimensions in the continental comparison.

The governance analysis revealed another important gap. Enabling Conditions recorded an average score of 42.48, compared with 13.69 for AI Policy and 9.31 for Civil Society Organisation (CSO) Engagement.

This suggests that the presence of foundational conditions does not necessarily correspond to equally strong policy development or civil society engagement.

The scatter plot comparing enabling conditions with AI policy also showed a correlation of approximately **0.34**. This indicates a modest positive association in the analysed data, rather than a strong relationship.

At the regional level, North Africa recorded the highest average score at 27.07, followed by West Africa at 24.76 and Southern Africa at 23.74. Central Africa recorded the lowest regional average at 9.88.

The indicator-level analysis further highlighted areas requiring attention. Public Disclosure of Government Algorithmic Systems recorded 0.00, Labour Protections scored 2.84, and Civil Society Engagement in Trust and Safety scored 5.19.

These findings suggest that responsible AI readiness should be approached as a multidimensional challenge. Improvements in foundational conditions need to be accompanied by stronger policy frameworks, transparency, inclusion, and meaningful stakeholder participation.

## 3. Understanding the Dashboard

The Power BI report contains five interconnected pages designed to move from a continent-wide overview to more detailed analysis.

### Page 1: Overview

The Overview page provides a high-level picture of responsible AI readiness across Africa.

It includes:

- Overall African GIRAI score.
- Selected GIRAI score.
- Highest- and lowest-performing countries.
- Average scores across the five dimensions.
- A geographical map showing GIRAI scores by country.
- Country rankings.
- Regional performance comparisons.
- Filters for country, income group, and subregion.

**Purpose:** To help users understand the distribution of responsible AI readiness across African countries and quickly identify countries or regions that warrant closer examination.

### Page 2: Dimension Analysis

The Dimension Analysis page compares the five responsible AI dimensions.

It includes:

- Average scores for the five dimensions.
- The strongest and weakest dimensions.
- A table comparing the strongest and weakest dimensions by country.
- A country-level dimension heatmap.
- Filters that allow users to explore specific countries and groups.

**Purpose:** To identify the areas where responsible AI readiness appears relatively strong and where the largest gaps exist.

### Page 3: Governance and Enabling Conditions

The Governance page examines the relationship between enabling conditions, AI policy, and civil society engagement.

It includes:

- A scatter plot comparing enabling conditions with AI policy.
- A comparison of AI Policy, CSO Engagement, and Enabling Conditions.
- The ten weakest indicators in Africa.
- A comparison of digital skills, cybersecurity, gender equality, and rule of law.
- An annotation highlighting countries with stronger enabling conditions but weaker AI policy scores.

**Purpose:** To examine whether the foundational conditions associated with AI readiness are accompanied by policy development and civil society participation.

### Page 4: Country Profile

The Country Profile page provides a more detailed view of an individual country.

It includes:

- The selected country's GIRAI score.
- Country ranking fields.
- Variance from the African average.
- Performance relative to the average.
- Dimension scores compared with African dimension averages.
- Governance pillar scores.
- Top and bottom indicators.

**Purpose:** To allow users to move beyond continental averages and examine the particular strengths and gaps of individual countries.

The ranking fields should be interpreted as meaningful only when a specific country is selected and the relevant ranking measures return valid results.

### Page 5: Insights and Recommendations

The final page brings the analysis together by highlighting important findings and potential areas for intervention.

**Purpose:** To connect the dashboard's analytical results with practical recommendations for policymakers, institutions, and other stakeholders.

Together, these pages provide a structured analytical experience, moving from the overall picture to dimensions, governance, country-level performance, and potential actions.

## 4. Top Five Insights and Recommendations

### Insight 1: Responsible AI Readiness Varies Significantly Across Countries

**What the data shows**

The overall GIRAI score for the African countries in our analysis averaged 21.79.

Nigeria recorded the highest score at 45.93, followed by:

| Country | GIRAI Score |
|---|---:|
| Nigeria | 45.93 |
| Egypt | 41.26 |
| Kenya | 39.53 |
| Ghana | 38.43 |
| Benin | 37.01 |

At the other end of the ranking, South Sudan recorded the lowest score at 4.04, followed by Chad at 6.51, Burundi at 8.70, Congo at 8.73, and the Central African Republic at 9.44.

The difference between Nigeria and South Sudan was 41.89 score points.

**What this means**

The results demonstrate that responsible AI readiness is not uniform across the countries examined.

Some countries score considerably higher than others, indicating differences in the index dimensions and indicators used to evaluate responsible AI readiness.

However, an overall score does not explain every aspect of a country's performance. Two countries with similar overall scores may have very different strengths and weaknesses across individual dimensions.

**Recommendations**

1. Use country profiles to identify the dimensions contributing to lower overall scores.
2. Develop country-specific improvement plans rather than applying the same intervention to every country.
3. Encourage collaboration and knowledge-sharing between countries with different strengths.
4. Investigate the policies, institutions, and initiatives associated with stronger indicator performance.
5. Track future GIRAI results to assess whether countries are making measurable progress.

**Priority:** High.

The objective should be to understand the drivers of performance and address specific gaps, rather than relying on rankings alone.

## 7. Methodology

The project followed a structured analytical process designed to transform index data into an interactive report.

### Step 1: Define the Analytical Problem

We began by framing the central question:

**How prepared are African countries to develop and govern AI responsibly, and which areas require the greatest attention?**

This question informed the selection of key indicators, dashboard pages, and analytical comparisons.

### Step 2: Examine the Data

The project used data from the Global Index on Responsible AI (GIRAI) 2026.

The dashboard focuses on 39 African countries and includes overall country scores, dimension-level measures, governance indicators, regional classifications, and income-group information.

The analysis was organised around the five responsible AI dimensions and three governance pillars shown in the dashboard.

### Step 3: Prepare and Structure the Data

Data preparation is important when combining country scores, dimension scores, governance indicators, and regional classifications.

Relevant validation activities for this type of project include:

- Checking column names and data types.
- Verifying country names and geographical classifications.
- Checking for missing values and duplicate records.
- Confirming that scores are stored as numeric values.
- Ensuring that country, region, income group, dimension, and indicator fields are consistently represented.
- Checking that country-level and aggregate-level values are not unintentionally mixed.
- Confirming that averages and rankings use the appropriate populations.

These are recommended validation activities. The specific cleaning operations completed should be documented according to the actual Power BI workflow.

### Step 4: Develop the Analytical Measures

The dashboard presents the following analytical measures:

- Overall GIRAI score.
- Selected GIRAI score.
- African average score.
- Country rankings.
- Dimension averages.
- Regional averages.
- Governance pillar averages.
- Variance from the African average.
- Performance relative to the average.
- Correlation between enabling conditions and AI policy.
- Top and bottom indicator scores.

The measures support comparisons across countries, dimensions, regions, and governance areas.

Where DAX was used, the corresponding measures should be documented with their calculation logic to improve transparency and reproducibility.

### Step 5: Build the Visualisations

The dashboard uses several visualisation types to communicate the results.

**KPI cards:** Present headline scores and performance summaries.

**Geographical map:** Displays country-level GIRAI scores across Africa.

**Horizontal bar charts:** Compare country rankings, regional scores, dimension scores, and indicator values.

**Scatter plot:** Examines the relationship between enabling conditions and AI policy.

**Heatmap:** Allows users to compare dimension-level performance across countries.

**Tables:** Present country scores, rankings, strongest dimensions, weakest dimensions, and indicator results.

The choice of visualisation depends on the analytical question. Rankings support comparisons, heatmaps reveal patterns across multiple dimensions, and scatter plots help explore relationships between variables.

### Step 6: Create Interactive Filtering

The dashboard includes filters for:

- Country.
- Income group.
- Subregion.

These filters allow users to explore subsets of the data and examine how the results change under different selections.

A Clear Filters button also helps users return to the default view.

Interactive filtering improves exploration, but it also makes measure validation important. Aggregate values and rankings should respond correctly to selections.

### Step 7: Interpret the Findings

We examined the resulting visualisations to identify:

- The highest- and lowest-scoring countries.
- The strongest and weakest responsible AI dimensions.
- Regional performance differences.
- Differences between enabling conditions and AI policy.
- Areas of weakness among the displayed indicators.
- Potential priorities for further investigation.

The findings were then translated into recommendations for policymakers and other stakeholders.

### Step 8: Communicate Insights

The dashboard was organised into five pages, moving from the overall picture to dimension analysis, governance, country profiles, and recommendations.

This structure supports a data storytelling approach: present the context, identify the patterns, investigate the gaps, and communicate potential actions.

## 9. Limitations and Considerations

A responsible analysis should make clear what the data can establish and where additional evidence is needed.

### 9.1 Index Scores Do Not Capture Every Aspect of AI Readiness

GIRAI scores provide a structured basis for comparison, but an index cannot represent every institutional, political, economic, technological, or social factor that influences responsible AI readiness.

**Consideration:** Interpret the scores alongside relevant national policies, institutional context, and other evidence.

### 9.2 Aggregate Scores Can Conceal Important Differences

A country may score relatively well overall while performing poorly on an individual dimension or indicator.

Similarly, regional averages can conceal differences between countries within the same region.

**Consideration:** Use country profiles and dimension-level analysis rather than relying exclusively on overall rankings.

### 9.3 Correlation Does Not Establish Causation

The correlation of approximately 0.34 between Enabling Conditions and AI Policy indicates an association in the analysed data.

It does not establish that improvements in enabling conditions cause stronger AI policy.

**Consideration:** Further research would be required to examine the mechanisms behind this relationship and assess other potentially relevant factors.

### 9.4 A Low or Zero Score Requires Methodological Context

The Public Disclosure of Government Algorithmic Systems indicator recorded a score of 0.00 in the displayed analysis.

However, a score of zero should not automatically be interpreted as proof that no relevant policy, practice, or activity exists.

Its meaning depends on the index definition, scoring rules, source evidence, and aggregation method.

**Consideration:** Consult the underlying GIRAI documentation before making specific claims about what a low or zero score means in practice.

### 9.5 The Dashboard Identifies Patterns, Not Definitive Causes

The dashboard identifies differences in country scores, regional performance, and governance indicators.

It does not independently establish why these differences exist.

For example, the lower regional average for Central Africa does not prove that any single institutional or economic factor caused the result.

**Consideration:** Use the dashboard to identify questions for further investigation, supported by additional evidence where possible.

### 9.6 Rankings Depend on the Selected Population and Scoring Framework

The rankings in the dashboard concern the African countries included in the analysis.

They should not be presented as global rankings unless the underlying dataset and ranking calculations support that interpretation.

**Consideration:** Clearly label rankings by geographical scope and validate them against the source data.

### 9.7 The Analysis Reflects the Available Dataset

The dashboard uses the GIRAI 2026 data available to the project.

Changes to the underlying index methodology, data coverage, country classifications, or source evidence could affect comparisons over time.

**Consideration:** Document the source version and review comparability before interpreting changes between different releases.

## 12. Conclusion

The *Responsible AI Readiness in Africa* project demonstrates how data analytics can be used to investigate a complex governance challenge.

Our analysis of 39 African countries revealed considerable differences in responsible AI readiness, a comparatively low score for Inclusion and Diversity, and substantial gaps between Enabling Conditions, AI Policy, and CSO Engagement.

The indicator-level results also highlighted areas requiring further investigation, particularly public disclosure of government algorithmic systems, labour protections, and civil society participation.

These findings suggest that responsible AI readiness should not be assessed solely through technological progress or foundational capacity. Policy implementation, transparency, inclusion, accountability, and public participation also matter.
